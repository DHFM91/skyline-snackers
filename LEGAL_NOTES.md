# IP notes

These are working notes on how this project stays on the safe side of copyright and
trademark. They are practical guidance, not legal advice. If the game is ever sold,
or a rights-holder sends a letter, talk to an actual IP lawyer.

## The core principles

1. **Ideas and game mechanics aren't protected; specific expression is.** Climbing
   buildings, punching windows, eating people, the army escalating, a world tour:
   all of that is fair game. The *Rampage* name, its art, sprites, sounds, music, and its
   specific characters (George, Lizzie, Ralph) are not.
2. **Never use another game's name as our title or repo name.** "Rampage" is a
   trademark owned by Warner Bros. Our title is **Skyline Snackers**. Don't put
   "Rampage" in the repo name, the store page or the marketing. Saying "inspired by
   classic arcade monster games" is fine.
3. **Everything is drawn and synthesized from scratch.** The project contains no ripped
   sprites, fan art, stock images or sampled audio.
4. **Public domain characters can be used by name. Protected characters get an
   original parody stand-in** with a new name and a deliberately different design,
   so the nod is clear but nothing protected is copied.

## Checklist before adding a new homage

1. **Is the source public domain?**
   - In the US, works published in **1930 or earlier** are PD as of 2026 (each Jan 1, another year enters).
   - Folklore, myth and legend are PD: Kraken, Jersey Devil, Wendigo, Mothman (as folklore), Gorgons, the Golem, Nessie, Yeti.
   - Classic novels: Frankenstein's creature (1818), Dracula (1897), The War of the Worlds tripods (1898), Mr. Hyde (1886).
   - **Trap:** a PD *story* can have a protected *film design*. Frankenstein's creature is PD, but
     Universal's flat head and neck bolts look is still theirs. Use the novel version or invent your own.
2. **If it's not PD,** make an original stand-in: a new name, a new silhouette and palette, and drop the
   one or two most iconic visual elements. If someone could mistake it for the real character,
   it's too close.
3. **Be especially careful with aggressive rights-holders.** Toho (Godzilla and kin) and
   major film studios actively enforce. Keep any kaiju-lizard homage generic and give it a clearly different name and look.
4. **Never use real logos, wordmarks or quotes** from the source.
5. **Update the table above** whenever you add a monster.

### Lovecraft quick reference (US publication year → PD status in 2026)

- *Dagon* (1919), *Nyarlathotep* (1920), *The Call of Cthulhu* (1928), *The Dunwich Horror* (1929): **public domain**.
- *The Whisperer in Darkness* (1931, Mi-Go): PD from Jan 2027.
- *At the Mountains of Madness* and *The Shadow over Innsmouth* (both 1936; shoggoths, Deep Ones): PD from Jan 2032.
  These later works' copyright status is murky and often treated as lapsed, but waiting is the conservative choice.

## Fair use / parody

Parody can be a defense in the US, but it is a defense you argue *after* being
challenged, not a permission. The strategy above (PD names, plus original stand-ins for
everything else) is meant so that we never need to argue it.
